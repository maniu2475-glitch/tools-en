About this tool

Training by heart rate turns a vague "run easy today" into a precise number. This calculator converts your age and resting heart rate into the five standard training zones using the Karvonen method, which works from your heart-rate reserve — the gap between your maximum and resting rates — rather than a flat percentage of maximum.

Zone 1 is recovery pace, Zone 2 is the conversational endurance and fat-burning zone where most training time belongs, Zone 3 builds aerobic capacity, Zone 4 is tempo and threshold work, and Zone 5 is short all-out intervals. You can choose between the classic 220-minus-age maximum or the more modern Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age), which fits older adults better.

Measure your resting heart rate first thing in the morning before getting up — most adults sit between 60 and 100 bpm, and well-trained endurance athletes can be below 50. Zone boundaries are guides, not walls: heat, caffeine, stress and fatigue all nudge your heart rate around. All calculations happen locally in your browser.

FAQ

Which max HR formula should I pick?

Tanaka (208 − 0.7 × age) is generally more accurate, especially over 40; the classic 220 − age is simpler but tends to overestimate for younger and underestimate for older people.

How do I measure my resting heart rate?

Count your pulse for 60 seconds right after waking, before coffee or movement. Average it over a few days for a stable number.

Is the fat-burning zone a myth?

You burn a higher fat percentage in Zone 2 but more total calories in higher zones. For fat loss, total energy balance matters more than which zone you sit in.
