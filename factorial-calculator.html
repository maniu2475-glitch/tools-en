About this tool

The factorial of a number n, written n!, is the product of all whole numbers from 1 up to n: 5! = 5×4×3×2×1 = 120. By definition 0! = 1. Factorials grow astonishingly fast — 10! is already 3,628,800, and 70! exceeds the number of atoms in the observable universe. That explosive growth is why factorials sit at the heart of combinatorics: the number of ways to arrange n distinct objects in a row is exactly n!.

This calculator computes factorials with BigInt arithmetic, so every digit is exact rather than floating-point approximated. The upper limit of 170! is imposed by practicality: it is the largest factorial whose digit count stays sane for display, at just over 306 digits. The result box also shows the digit count and the number of trailing zeros — a classic maths puzzle, since trailing zeros come from factors of 5 pairing with factors of 2.

Students use it to check permutation and combination homework, and programmers use it to sanity-check BigInt implementations. For related maths, try our prime number checker, LCM & GCD calculator and quadratic equation solver. Everything runs locally in your browser.

FAQ

Why is 0! equal to 1?

It is defined that way so the pattern n! = n × (n−1)! keeps working at n=1, and so counting formulas like the empty arrangement (exactly one way to arrange nothing) stay consistent.

Why does it stop at 170?

Beyond that the result is still computable with BigInt, but 170! is the largest factorial below a googol×googol — the cap keeps the page responsive. Larger values can be computed in scientific notation if needed.

How do I count trailing zeros of n! by hand?

Sum floor(n/5) + floor(n/25) + floor(n/125) + ... — Legendre's formula. The calculator applies it automatically.
