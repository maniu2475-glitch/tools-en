About this tool

Whether you are planning a weight-loss deficit or just curious how much that long walk was worth, this calculator estimates the calories you burn for over fifteen common activities. It uses the standard MET method endorsed by exercise science: one MET is the energy you burn sitting still, and each activity has a measured multiple of it.

The formula is kcal = MET × 3.5 × weight (kg) ÷ 200 × minutes. Heavier people burn more for the same activity, and intensity matters enormously — running at 13 km/h burns roughly three times what a slow walk does per minute. A 70 kg person jogging for 30 minutes burns about 294 kcal.

Treat the result as an honest estimate, not gospel: individual efficiency, terrain and fitness all shift the real number by 10–20%. FAQ

What is a MET?

A Metabolic Equivalent of Task. 1 MET ≈ 1 kcal per kg per hour at rest. An activity rated 8 METs burns about 8× your resting rate.

How accurate is this?

Typically within 10–20% for steady activities. Intervals, hills and fitness level cause real burn to vary.

Why does weight matter?

Moving more mass costs more energy, so a 100 kg runner burns about 43% more calories than a 70 kg runner over the same session.
