About this tool

Standard deviation measures how spread out a set of numbers is around their mean. A low standard deviation means the values cluster tightly around the average; a high one means they are widely scattered. It is the single most used measure of dispersion in statistics, appearing everywhere from quality control and finance (where it underlies volatility and risk) to test scores and scientific measurement error.

There are two versions. The sample standard deviation (dividing by n−1) is used when your numbers are a sample from a larger population — it corrects for the tendency of samples to underestimate spread. The population standard deviation (dividing by n) applies when you have every value in the whole population. This calculator shows both so you can use whichever your course or field requires.

The output also includes the mean, variance (the square of standard deviation), median, range, and the minimum and maximum values, giving you a complete descriptive-statistics summary in one pass. Everything is computed in your browser with plain JavaScript — your data never leaves your device, which matters when analysing confidential figures.

FAQ

Should I use sample or population standard deviation?

Use sample (n−1) when your data is a sample drawn from a bigger population — the usual case in surveys and experiments. Use population (n) only when you have all values of the entire group.

Can standard deviation be negative?

No. It is the square root of variance, so it is always zero or positive. Zero means every value is identical.

What is the 68-95-99.7 rule?

For roughly normal data, about 68% of values fall within one standard deviation of the mean, 95% within two, and 99.7% within three.
