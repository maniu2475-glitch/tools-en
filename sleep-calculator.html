About this tool

A good night's sleep is not just about duration — it is about waking at the right moment. Each night you pass through several sleep cycles of roughly 90 minutes each, moving between light sleep, deep sleep and REM. If your alarm rings in the middle of a deep stage, you feel groggy no matter how long you slept. If it rings near the end of a cycle, you wake up clear-headed.

This sleep calculator takes the number of minutes you usually need to fall asleep, then counts back from your wake-up time in full 90-minute cycles. It suggests six bedtimes, from six cycles (about nine hours) down to three cycles (about four and a half hours), so you can pick the one that fits your evening. Most adults do best on five cycles — seven and a half hours.

The tool is a planning aid, not a medical device: real cycles vary between 80 and 120 minutes from person to person and night to night. Use the suggestions as a starting point and adjust by 15 minutes if you consistently wake groggy. Everything runs locally in your browser; no data is uploaded.

FAQ

How many hours of sleep do adults need?

Most adults need 7–9 hours. Five 90-minute cycles gives 7.5 hours, which sits comfortably in that range.

Why 90 minutes per cycle?

90 minutes is the average length of one full sleep cycle. Waking near the end of a cycle, rather than in deep sleep, is what makes mornings feel easier.

What if I can't fall asleep quickly?

Adjust the "minutes to fall asleep" field. 10–20 minutes is typical; if you drop off instantly you may be sleep-deprived.
