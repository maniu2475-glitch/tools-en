About this tool

Roman numerals use seven letters — I, V, X, L, C, D and M — to represent values 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, 500 and 1000. The subtractive notation means a smaller letter before a larger one is subtracted: IV is 4, IX is 9, XL is 40. Despite being thousands of years old, Roman numerals still appear everywhere: book chapters, film sequels, clock faces, Super Bowl numbering, monarch names and outline headings.

This converter works in both directions. Type a number such as 2026 and get MMXXVI; paste MCMLXXXIV and get 1984. The parser validates input strictly, so malformed numerals like IIII or VX are rejected with a clear error rather than returning a wrong answer. The supported range is the standard 1-3999, which covers all classical usage.

Because everything is plain JavaScript running locally, results are instant and private. Students use it to check homework, writers use it for headings, and tattoo designers use it to double-check dates before inking them permanently — a mistake there is hard to fix.

FAQ

What is the largest Roman numeral?

Standard notation supports up to 3999 (MMMCMXCIX). Larger numbers used overlines, which this tool does not support.

Is IIII valid?

No. The subtractive form IV is the standard for 4, though some old clocks do show IIII.

Can I use lowercase?

Yes. The parser accepts i, v, x, l, c, d, m and treats them the same as uppercase.
