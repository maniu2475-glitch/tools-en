About this tool

"What should I weigh?" has no single answer, but medicine has produced several formulas over the decades. This calculator shows your ideal body weight under all four classics at once, so you can see how much they agree — and they agree more than you might expect.

Devine (1974) was designed for drug dosing and remains the most cited. Robinson (1983) and Miller (1983) were revisions of Devine using newer population data, while Hamwi (1964) came from diabetic nutrition work. Each starts from a base weight at 152 cm (5 ft) — about 50 kg for men, 45.5 kg for women — and adds roughly 0.9–1.2 kg per extra inch of height.

Note that these formulas ignore muscle mass, frame size and age, so athletes often run "overweight" by them while being perfectly healthy. Today most clinicians prefer BMI ranges or waist measurements; the calculator also shows the weight that equals BMI 25 at your height as a sanity check. FAQ

Which formula is most accurate?

Devine is the most widely used clinically, but none is "accurate" for everyone — they estimate population averages, not you personally.

Do these formulas apply to children?

No. They assume adults; children and teens need growth-chart percentiles instead.

What if I'm very muscular?

Muscle weighs more than fat per volume, so muscular people often exceed ideal-weight formulas while staying healthy. BMI has the same limitation.
