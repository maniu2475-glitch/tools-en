About this tool

Ohm's law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage across it and inversely proportional to its resistance, written as V = I × R. Combined with Joule's law for electrical power, P = V × I, you get a compact set of equations that lets you derive any unknown quantity from any two known ones. This calculator applies all of them automatically, including the less obvious combinations such as power plus resistance, which require a square root.

Typical uses: sizing a current-limiting resistor for an LED (for a 5 V supply and a 2 V LED at 20 mA, R = (5−2)/0.02 = 150 Ω), checking that a 12 V / 2 A power supply can drive a 5 Ω load (it needs 2.4 A, so it cannot), or working out how much heat a resistor will dissipate before choosing its wattage rating. Always pick a resistor rated at least twice the calculated power dissipation for a comfortable safety margin.

The calculator assumes ideal components at DC or low frequency, and positive values only. For AC circuits with capacitors and inductors you also need reactance and impedance, and for very high frequencies or long wires the assumption of a purely resistive load breaks down. Values are shown to four decimal places; the underlying math uses full double precision. Everything runs client-side, so it works offline and is safe for confidential engineering numbers.
