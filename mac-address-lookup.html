About the MAC Address Lookup & Formatter

Every network card carries a 48-bit MAC address whose first 24 bits (the OUI) are assigned by the IEEE to a specific manufacturer. This tool splits any MAC address into its OUI and NIC parts, looks the prefix up in a bundled subset of the IEEE registry, and tells you the vendor. It also decodes the two special bits in the first octet that people often trip over: the multicast bit and the locally administered bit - the latter is set on randomised (privacy) MAC addresses used by phones and on virtual adapters, which is why those addresses never resolve to a vendor. Finally it converts the same address into every format you may need: colon-separated for Linux and macOS, hyphenated for Windows, Cisco dotted quad, bare hex for scripts and databases, and the EUI-64 form used for IPv6 link-local addresses (with the FFFE insertion and the U/L bit flip). Useful for network troubleshooting, switch and router configuration, inventory work and security analysis. Everything happens in your browser; no address is uploaded.
