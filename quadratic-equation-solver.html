About this tool

A quadratic equation is any equation of the form ax² + bx + c = 0 with a ≠ 0. Its solutions — the points where the parabola crosses the x-axis — are found with the quadratic formula x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a. This solver computes the discriminant D = b² − 4ac first, because it tells the whole story at a glance: D > 0 means two distinct real roots, D = 0 means one repeated real root (the vertex touches the axis), and D < 0 means the roots are a complex-conjugate pair, which this tool reports in full a ± bi form. It also reports the vertex of the parabola at x = −b/2a, useful for maximum-or-minimum word problems in physics and economics. Typical applications include projectile-motion problems in physics, break-even analysis in business, and optimizing dimensions when area or perimeter is fixed. The calculation is exact to floating-point precision and runs entirely client-side, so nothing you enter is transmitted anywhere.
