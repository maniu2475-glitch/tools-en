About this tool

Base32 is a binary-to-text encoding that represents data with a 32-character alphabet (A-Z and 2-7), exactly as defined in RFC 4648. It is used in TOTP two-factor authentication secrets (the codes you scan into Google Authenticator), DNSSEC keys, and systems where case-insensitive handling or avoiding confusing characters matters. Base32 is less dense than Base64 - every 5 bytes become 8 characters - but it survives being read aloud, printed, or re-typed much better. This tool encodes and decodes arbitrary UTF-8 text, tolerates lowercase input and missing padding, and reports invalid characters clearly. All computation happens in your browser, so secrets stay private.
