About this tool

A leap year exists because the Earth does not orbit the Sun in a whole number of days — the tropical year is about 365.2422 days, so without occasional extra days the calendar would drift by roughly one day every four years and, over centuries, Easter would wander into summer. The Gregorian reform of 1582 fixed the older Julian over-correction with a two-step rule that this checker applies exactly: a year is a leap year if it is divisible by 4, except century years (divisible by 100), which are not leap years — unless they are also divisible by 400. So 2024 and 2028 are leap years, 1900 was not, while 2000 was. The checker tells you the verdict for any year you type, explains which part of the rule fired, and lists the nearest previous and next leap years so you can plan 29-February birthdays or quarterly deadlines. Software engineers use it to sanity-check date libraries, since off-by-one leap-year bugs are notorious in billing and interest calculations. Everything is pure client-side JavaScript — no request is made for something as simple as dividing by four.
