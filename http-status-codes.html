About this tool

HTTP status codes are the three-digit numbers a server sends back with every response, and they are the fastest way to tell whether a request worked, got redirected, or failed — and, crucially, whose fault it was. The first digit groups them: 1xx means the request is still being processed, 2xx means success, 3xx means the client should look somewhere else, 4xx means the client made a mistake, and 5xx means the server did. That split is the single most useful debugging rule: a 404 or 422 is something to fix in your request, URL or payload, while a 500 or 502 is something to fix on the server or in your infrastructure. Common traps worth memorising: 301 versus 302 (permanent versus temporary redirect — search engines carry link equity through 301 only), 401 versus 403 (not authenticated versus authenticated but not allowed), 429 (rate limited — back off and retry with exponential delay), and 502 versus 504 (bad response from upstream versus no response in time). This reference lists the standard codes from the IANA registry in a filterable table; type a number, a phrase or a keyword such as "redirect" or "timeout" to narrow it down. For checking what your own site actually returns, pair this with a curl request, or use the URL parser and user-agent parser tools to inspect the request side.
