Phone Number Formatter

Paste a phone number in any style and get it cleaned up into E.164, international and national formats, plus a quick sanity check on its length.

About this tool

Phone numbers are stored in dozens of shapes: (415) 555-2671, 415.555.2671, +1 415 555 2671, 0044 20 7946 0958. APIs that accept SMS or two-factor verification essentially always want E.164 — a plus sign, the country calling code, and the subscriber number with no spaces, dashes or brackets, capped at fifteen digits. Normalising input before sending it prevents the very common class of bug where a perfectly valid number is rejected because of a stray space or a trunk prefix.

This formatter strips every non-digit character, converts a leading 00 international prefix into +, removes a single leading trunk zero before applying the selected country code, and then renders the result in four common shapes: E.164, a separated international form, the national form with a locally appropriate grouping, and an RFC 3966 tel: URI that can go straight into a link or a call-tracking system.

The length check is a heuristic, not a full numbering-plan validator. Subscriber numbers are typically 6 to 12 digits after the country code, so the tool warns when the total length looks too short or too long. National grouping is also approximate: it follows each country's most common convention rather than the full per-region rules that a library such as libphonenumber applies, and it does not know about area-code reassignment or toll-free ranges.

If you need strict validation, do it server-side with a numbering-plan database and treat this page as a quick cleanup and visual check. Nothing you type leaves your browser — the formatting logic is plain JavaScript running locally, which makes it safe to paste real customer numbers while preparing data for import.
