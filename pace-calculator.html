About this tool

Pace is the runner's core metric: how many minutes and seconds it takes to cover one kilometre or one mile. Unlike speed, pace maps directly onto race strategy — if you want to finish a marathon under four hours, you need a pace of about 5:41 per kilometre, and holding that rhythm is the whole game.

This calculator works in all directions. Type a finish time and a distance and it returns your average pace in both per-km and per-mile terms. Quick buttons load the classic race distances: 5K, 10K, half marathon (21.0975 km) and full marathon (42.195 km). The unit toggle lets you think in kilometres or miles, whichever your training plan uses.

Runners use pace calculators for goal setting (what pace do I need?), post-race analysis (what pace did I actually hold?), and even splits planning. Because it is a single static page running entirely in your browser, it loads fast on a phone at the race start line and never sends your data anywhere.

FAQ

How do I hit a sub-2-hour half marathon?

You need a pace of roughly 5:41 min/km (9:09 min/mile) — enter 21.0975 km and 1:59:59 to see it exactly.

Can I use it for walking or cycling?

Yes. The math is unit-agnostic; cyclists usually think in speed instead, but the pace output is still correct.

What is the difference between pace and speed?

Speed is distance per hour (e.g. 10 km/h); pace is time per distance (e.g. 6:00 per km). They contain the same information, but pace is easier to run by.
