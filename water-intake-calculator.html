About this tool

Water makes up about 60% of your body weight and every system depends on it — temperature control, joint lubrication, nutrient transport and waste removal. Yet most people guess their daily needs. This calculator turns your weight, activity level and climate into a concrete number of liters to drink each day.

The estimate starts from the widely used guideline of 35 ml per kilogram of body weight (about 2.5 liters for a 70 kg adult — a little more than the classic "8 glasses" rule). It then adds roughly 350 ml for every 30 minutes of exercise to cover sweat losses, and an extra allowance for hot, humid weather or cold, high-altitude conditions where you lose more water through breath and skin.

Remember that about 20% of fluid normally comes from food, so you do not need to drink every drop. Thirst is still your best daily guide, and needs rise with fever, breastfeeding or heavy labor. The calculation runs entirely in your browser.

FAQ

Is 8 glasses a day enough?

8 glasses is about 2 liters — fine for many people, but needs scale with body size, activity and climate, which is exactly what this calculator accounts for.

Can I drink too much water?

Yes — drinking extreme amounts in a short time can dilute blood sodium (hyponatremia). Spread intake through the day rather than forcing liters at once.

Does coffee count?

Caffeinated drinks are mildly diuretic but still net-positive for hydration. Water, milk and juice all count toward your total.
