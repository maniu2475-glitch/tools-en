About this tool

Your basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the energy your body needs just to stay alive: breathing, circulating blood, maintaining body temperature and keeping your brain running. It typically accounts for 60-70% of the calories you burn in a day, which is why knowing it is the starting point of any weight plan. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, published in 1990, which research has repeatedly shown to be the most accurate general-purpose formula for healthy adults — more reliable than the older Harris-Benedict equation.

BMR alone is not what you actually burn in a day. Multiply it by an activity factor to get your TDEE (total daily energy expenditure): 1.2 for a desk-bound lifestyle, 1.375 for light exercise 1-3 days a week, 1.55 for moderate exercise, 1.725 for hard training, and 1.9 for a physically demanding job plus daily training. The result box shows all five levels so you can see the full range at once.

Use your TDEE as the baseline: eat around it to maintain weight, about 500 kcal below it to lose roughly half a kilogram per week, or above it to gain. FAQ

Which BMR formula is best?

Mifflin-St Jeor is considered the most accurate for most people. The classic Harris-Benedict formula overestimates by around 5%. This tool uses Mifflin-St Jeor.

Why does muscle matter?

Muscle tissue burns more calories at rest than fat, so muscular people have a higher BMR than the formula predicts. Formulas are estimates — track real-world results over a few weeks.

Is BMR the same as calories burned at rest during the day?

Not exactly. BMR is measured under strict fasting, resting conditions. Everyday resting burn (RMR) is slightly higher, typically within 10% of BMR.
