About the Typing Speed Test

This test measures gross WPM (all words typed) and net WPM (corrected for errors) over a 60-second window using the standard five-keystroke-per-word convention. Accuracy is the percentage of correctly typed characters, the two numbers recruiters and typing certifications care about most.

Average adult typing speed is around 40 WPM; professional typists reach 65–75 WPM and beyond. Practicing in short focused bursts with immediate accuracy feedback is the proven way to improve — retake this test daily and track your net WPM trend rather than chasing raw speed at the expense of mistakes.

The whole test runs client-side: keystrokes are counted locally in JavaScript and never transmitted, so you can safely use it for practice, warm-ups or screening tests. Different texts are loaded each round so you cannot simply memorize the passage.

FAQ

What is a good typing speed?

40 WPM is typical for casual typists, 60+ is considered fast, and 75+ is professional-level. Accuracy above 95% matters more than raw speed.

Gross vs net WPM?

Gross WPM counts every word typed; net WPM subtracts incorrect words. Net WPM is the honest number employers look at.

Why 60 seconds?

One minute is long enough to smooth out bursts while staying focused; it's also the most common standardized test duration.
