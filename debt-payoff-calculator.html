Debt Payoff Calculator

Find out exactly when a balance will be paid off, how much interest you will pay in total, and how much time and money an extra monthly payment would save you. Works for credit cards, personal loans, auto loans and student loans.

How it works

  • Interest accrues monthly on the remaining balance.
  • A payment must exceed the monthly interest or the balance never falls.
  • Extra payments go entirely to principal.

Why calculating a payoff date changes behaviour

Revolving debt such as a credit card balance is psychologically slippery: the minimum payment always feels affordable, so the balance can sit untouched for years. A debt payoff calculator makes the cost concrete. Once you see that an $8,000 balance at 22.9% APR with $300 monthly payments takes nearly four years to clear and costs thousands in interest, the motivation to pay more becomes obvious. More importantly, the tool lets you experiment: adding $100 per month typically saves far more than $100 times the number of months, because every extra dollar reduces the balance that interest accrues on for all remaining months.

The mathematics behind the result

Each month the calculator computes interest as balance × APR / 12, adds it to the balance, then subtracts your payment. If the payment is smaller than the monthly interest, the balance grows and the loan can never be repaid — the tool detects this case and warns you instead of looping forever. Otherwise it simulates month by month until the balance reaches zero, counting months and accumulating interest paid. That gives three outputs: the number of months to be debt-free, the total interest paid, and the total amount paid including principal. Running the same simulation with and without the extra payment produces the savings figure.

Worked example

Take an $8,000 credit card balance at 22.9% APR. Paying $300 per month, the debt is cleared in about 39 months and roughly $3,600 of interest is paid on top of the original $8,000 — a total outlay near $11,600. Raise the payment to $400 per month and payoff drops to about 27 months with interest near $2,400, saving more than $1,200 and a full year. That disproportionate saving is exactly what the calculator is designed to make visible.

Practical strategy notes

If you have several debts, two strategies are common. Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first and minimises total interest paid; this calculator helps you evaluate each debt individually. Snowball targets the smallest balance first, which usually costs slightly more but delivers earlier psychological wins. Whichever you choose, always keep paying at least the minimum on every other debt so you avoid late fees and penalty rates. Also check whether your lender charges a prepayment penalty before aggressively overpaying a fixed-rate loan, and remember that a balance transfer to a 0% APR card can cut interest dramatically if — and only if — you clear the balance before the promotional period ends.
